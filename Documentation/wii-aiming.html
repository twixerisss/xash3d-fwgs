Aiming with the Wii remote pointer

SDL gives us the remote's IR sensor as an absolute pointer, not relative motion. The engine's mouse look expects deltas — so pointing worked until the pointer hit the edge of the screen, and then you simply couldn't turn any further. Here's what replaced it.

The screen is two regions

That split is the whole trick. A steady middle is what makes fine aiming possible, because small hand movements don't drag the whole world with them. Pushing toward an edge is how you swing the camera, and coming back to the middle is how you stop.

How fast it turns

The ramp restarts at the box edge rather than the screen centre, which is why crossing the line feels smooth instead of snapping into a turn. Pitch works the same way on the vertical axis, just slower by default.

The gun leans, the bullet doesn't

This is the one honest compromise. The lean is cosmetic. Making shots actually follow the pointer means firing down a different vector than the camera looks, which GoldSrc doesn't do natively — a real change to how firing works, not a tweak. Keeping the lean small means the gap never gets wide enough to notice.

What's mapped where

All five are archived cvars — change them from the console and they persist.
cvardefaultwhat it does
wii_ir1Pointer aiming on or off entirely
wii_ir_deadzone0.35How much of the screen is the steady zone, as a fraction. Raise it for a calmer centre, lower it to turn sooner
wii_ir_yawspeed220Degrees per second of horizontal turn at the screen edge
wii_ir_pitchspeed160The same for looking up and down
wii_ir_gunsway7Degrees the weapon leans toward the pointer

Movement stays on the nunchuk stick, which arrives as a normal joystick axis — so the nunchuk is required to walk, and the remote handles everything you aim.

Implemented in engine/platform/ogc/in_ogc.c. The lean is applied to clgame.viewent after the client dll has placed the weapon, so it works with any game dll rather than only stock Half-Life.
